SASP 2025 Pre-Conference Workshop

Title: Beyond Correlation: A Practical Introduction to Causal Inference in Observational Social Psychology
Presenter: Joseph Bulbulia
Event: SASP 2025 Pre-Conference Workshop
Date: 19 November 2025
Location: University of Melbourne

Workshop website and downloadable materials

Workshop Materials

The workshop site includes logistics, notes, setup instructions, and hand-outs.

Open Workshop Site Workshop Repository

Abstract: This full-day workshop introduced causal inference for observational social psychology, with an emphasis on clearly formulated causal questions, identification assumptions, doubly robust estimation, and heterogeneous treatment effects. The workshop site includes lecture notes, exercises, and setup instructions for the causalworkshop package.

Keywords: causal inference, observational social psychology, workshop, heterogeneous treatment effects, teaching, R

Student Presentations

Event: SASP 2025 Pre-Conference Workshop — Beyond Correlation: A Practical Introduction to Causal Inference in Observational Social Psychology Date: 19 November 2025 Location: University of Melbourne

Zahle Stewart: Does Employer Gratitude Improve Employee Well-being?

Student Presentation | Format: PDF Slides

Open Slides (PDF)

Abstract: This presentation asks whether higher perceived employer gratitude improves employee well-being one year later. It uses longitudinal data from the New Zealand Attitudes and Values Study (NZAVS) to estimate effects across health, psychological, social, and life-reflective outcomes.

Keywords: employer gratitude, employee well-being, causal inference, NZAVS, workplace appreciation

Hannah Robinson: Religion and Personality

Student Presentation | Format: PDF Slides

Open Slides (PDF)

Abstract: This presentation investigates the causal relationship between religious service attendance and personality traits, asking whether religious participation affects personality development.

Keywords: religion, personality, religious service attendance, causal inference, NZAVS

William Bier Interdisciplinary Award Lecture

Title: Who Benefits from Religious Attendance? Heterogeneous Causal Effects on Well-being and Cooperation in New Zealand
Presenter: Joseph Bulbulia
Event: William Bier Interdisciplinary Award, APA Division 36 Psychology of Religion
Date: 8 August 2025
Location: Denver, Colorado

Conference Presentation | Duration: ~50 minutes | Format: HTML Slides

Hosted on Dropbox as RevealJS HTML slides (best viewed on desktop; press S for speaker notes).

Open Slides (HTML)

Abstract: We examine how the causal effects of religious participation on well-being and cooperation vary across different population subgroups, linking two literatures on religious attendance that have developed largely independently: health and cooperation.

Keywords: causal inference, heterogeneous effects, religion, well-being, cooperation, longitudinal, panel, New Zealand

SPARCC Day 2: Average Treatment Effects

Title: Average Treatment Effects: Church Attendance and Cooperation
Presenter: Joseph Bulbulia
Event: SPARCC Day 2 Causal Inference Workshop
Date: 12 August 2025
Location: Seventh College, UC San Diego

Workshop Presentation | Duration: ~60 minutes | Format: PDF Slides

Open Slides (PDF)

SPARCC Day 2: Heterogeneous Treatment Effects

Title: Heterogeneous Treatment Effects: Who Benefits from Church Attendance?
Presenter: Joseph Bulbulia
Event: SPARCC Day 2 Causal Inference Workshop
Date: 12 August 2025
Location: Seventh College, UC San Diego

Workshop Presentation | Duration: ~60 minutes | Format: PDF Slides

Open Slides (PDF)

Abstract: This session moves from population averages to subgroup differences, using causal machine learning to ask how the effects of church attendance on well-being vary across people under the study’s causal assumptions.

Keywords: heterogeneous treatment effects, causal inference, church attendance, well-being, cooperation, machine learning, subgroup analysis, personalised treatment effects, New Zealand

Workshop Context

These presentations were delivered as part of SPARCC Day 2, a methods workshop on causal inference for studying religion, spirituality, and social behaviour using longitudinal data.
